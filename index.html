Smart Finance Advisor

SFA is a personal finance application for people who use several bank accounts and want to manage their finances more effectively. This page documents our team, the project and our progress during the course.

03

Problem

When spending is spread across several accounts, checking each banking app does not always make it easy to see the total or compare it with a monthly budget.

01

Spending across accounts

Total spending, recurring payments and budget progress can be difficult to review together.

02

Manual tracking

Collecting and categorizing data takes time before users can evaluate their spending.

03

Data privacy

Users need to understand what financial information they share and how it will be processed.

04

Solution and value proposition

We plan to bring transactions from supported bank accounts into SFA, group expenses by category and compare them with the user’s budget. Recommendations will explain which spending patterns may be worth reviewing.

The aim is to reduce the work needed to review spending across accounts and decide where to adjust a budget.
Project vision

Core functionality

  • Personal finance dashboard
  • Income and expense tracking
  • Expense categorization and spending analysis
  • Budget creation and monitoring
  • Basic financial insights and recommendations
Planned development

Future enhancements

  • Consent-based, read-only Open Banking integration
  • Automatic recurring-transaction detection
  • Personalized budget alerts and recommendations
  • AI-generated financial summaries
  • Improved data visualization and user experience

We have not selected an Open Banking provider yet. We need to check bank coverage and costs, explain what data is shared, and implement access revocation and deletion. We also want to limit the information sent to external AI services.

05

Customer segments

Primary segment

People in Romania who actively use at least two bank accounts.

They want to manage their spending without repeatedly consolidating their finances by hand.

Initial recruitment

We plan to start with students who have an income and people in their first years of work, using university and professional contacts to find interview participants.

We are starting with individual budgets. We may explore household budgeting later if interviews show a need for it.

07

Proposed competitive advantage

We want users to be able to check why SFA makes a recommendation, choose whether to follow it and review the result later.

  1. Identify

    Find a spending change or a budget that needs attention.

  2. Explain

    Show the period, transactions and calculation behind the suggestion.

  3. Choose

    Let the user correct the data, reject the suggestion or choose an adjustment.

  4. Review

    Compare later spending with the adjustment the user chose.

We need to find out whether this is more useful than the tools people already use. Competitors also offer bank connections and AI features, so those alone are not enough to set SFA apart.

01

Problem identification & initial solution

Our starting hypothesis was that using several financial accounts makes it harder to understand personal spending.

M1 framed the problem around fragmented information, manual consolidation and the difficulty of turning transaction history into useful decisions. This was a product hypothesis, not an established fact about everyone with several accounts. We used discovery to investigate when people actually need a total, how they obtain it and whether the effort matters to them.

Existing MVP · project baseline

What we initially proposed

A personal-finance dashboard combining statement imports from supported banks, custom periods, categories, budgets, recurring expenses and AI summaries.

These are the existing capabilities described by the project team. This research is not evidence that their usability or calculation accuracy has already been validated with customers.

Hypotheses · not validated

What we considered for v2

Open banking to reduce data entry, Financial Data Health to make imported data verifiable, and Smart Tags to connect spending to personal contexts.

The proposed subscription of approximately 20 RON/month, including the 19.99 RON concept in M1, remains a pricing hypothesis. The survey did not test purchases, retention or willingness to pay for SFA.

Question guiding discovery: when does an existing method fail to provide a useful answer, or require enough unwanted work that someone would change it?

02

Customer discovery plan & process

Our approach is to study recent behaviour before asking people to evaluate SFA: identify an actual spending question, reconstruct the current workflow, understand its consequences, and only then compare a solution on the same task.

  1. Explore current behaviour. An online questionnaire asks about the previous 30 days, the most recent attempt to calculate spending, tools, time, difficulty and account connections. Open questions also ask what worked well.
  2. Look for different behaviours. Cross-tabulate account count with checking frequency, method, time, difficulty and connection experience. Read free-text answers for both friction and successful alternatives.
  3. Add context without pooling sources. Review the supplied online comments and the team member’s account of classroom feedback. These are qualitative context, not additional questionnaire respondents.
  4. Investigate a narrower segment. Document individual interviews and observe the current workflow before showing SFA. Then test the MVP, correct totals and repeat use. These next activities are described below as planned work.

03

Questionnaire & analysis rules

The questionnaire focuses on what respondents did, rather than whether they like the idea of an AI finance app. It asks about successful workflows as well as difficulties.

Read all 13 questions — original Romanian wording

The export also contains a timestamp column. It is metadata, not an additional question. Method selection is multiple-choice; the other open questions retain the respondent’s own wording.

  1. Confirmi că ai cel puțin 18 ani, locuiești în România și ești de acord să participi în condițiile descrise mai sus?
  2. La câte bănci sau servicii financiare, precum Revolut, ai folosit efectiv conturi personale în ultimele 30 de zile?
  3. Pentru ce folosești conturile respective?
    Poți da unul-două exemple: un cont pentru salariu, altul pentru cumpărături sau economii. Poți menționa banca ori aplicația, fără numere de cont sau sume.
  4. În ultimele 30 de zile, de câte ori ai încercat să afli cât ai cheltuit în total într-o anumită perioadă?
    De exemplu, totalul unei săptămâni sau al unei luni.
  5. Ce te-a făcut să verifici atunci?
    Un exemplu în una-două propoziții este suficient.
  6. Cum ai încercat să afli totalul?
    Bifează metodele folosite în acea situație.
  7. Aproximativ cât timp ai petrecut cu verificarea, incluzând adunarea informațiilor?
  8. Cât de ușor sau dificil a fost să obții informația de care aveai nevoie?
  9. Ce a funcționat bine sau te-a încurcat în acea situație?
    Dacă a existat o dificultate, poți spune și ce ai făcut mai departe sau ce consecință a avut. Dacă totul a mers bine, spune ce ți-a fost util.
  10. Ce aplicații, tabele sau alte metode ai încercat până acum pentru a-ți urmări banii?
    Menționează, dacă îți amintești, numele lor și de ce le-ai păstrat sau ai renunțat. Dacă folosești doar aplicația băncii ori nu urmărești cheltuielile separat, poți spune asta.
  11. Ai conectat vreodată un cont bancar la o aplicație din afara băncii, pentru ca ea să îți afișeze soldul sau tranzacțiile?
    Ne referim la accesul la datele contului, nu la adăugarea cardului într-un portofel digital sau la plata unei cumpărături.
  12. Dacă ai luat în calcul conectarea unui cont la o astfel de aplicație, ce a contat în decizia ta?
  13. În ultimele trei luni, ai avut vreo întrebare despre cheltuielile tale la care metodele folosite nu te-au ajutat să răspunzi?
    Dacă da, povestește pe scurt situația și ce ai încercat. Dacă nu, poți scrie «nu».

How counts, denominators and qualitative themes were calculated
  • Frozen comparison base: R001–R102, the first 102 submissions. The raw export has 103 rows; R103 is disclosed separately. IDs refer to submissions, not independently verified unique identities.
  • Recent checks: 83/102 (81.4%) report at least one attempt in 30 days. We exclude 16 “never” and 3 “do not remember” answers from recent-task measures, while retaining their comments as qualitative material.
  • Main behavioural subset: 73 respondents both used two or more banks/services and reported a recent attempt. A question about a total does not establish that all their accounts were included.
  • Time: self-reported intervals include information gathering for that check. They do not separately measure earlier setup or ongoing data entry. We do not turn these intervals into a precise average.
  • Methods: multiple selections can overlap. We count each full option once and do not split the answer at every comma or infer missing selections from later comments.
  • Themes: each submission is counted once per theme across free-text answers. Themes can overlap and include past experiences. A mention is not proof of a current unmet need.
  • Inconsistencies: R006 reports zero services but describes Revolut; R060 reports one but names two. We preserve the original choices. Comparison with the previous analysis found no quantitative changes in the first 102 rows; four “NA” text values had previously appeared as blanks.

Other findings: connections, unanswered questions and the extra response

External account connections: 85/102 (83.3%) have not tried; 6/102 (5.9%) currently use one; 9/102 (8.8%) used one previously; 2/102 (2.0%) stopped before completion. These groups do not form a churn cohort. Among the 73 recent multi-service checkers, just 4 currently connect accounts; two describe PFA/company accounting, so this cannot validate a personal open-banking workflow.

Unanswered spending questions: 66/102 (64.7%) explicitly answer “no”, 25/102 (24.5%) leave the final question blank, 1/102 (1.0%) supplies a dash and 10/102 (9.8%) describe a situation or wish. These ten include needs beyond SFA’s current scope. “No” also does not erase friction described elsewhere: R053 reports abandoning a laborious spreadsheet and still answers “no”.

Abandonment: six submissions explicitly describe stopping a method. Four mention effort or recording transactions. Other explanations include no perceived improvement in money left over and already staying within budget. Stopping one tool does not demonstrate demand for another.

R103, added 31 October: describes difficulty combining category totals from two banks and the expected effort of Excel. It is a relevant qualitative case, but frequency is “do not remember”, so it is excluded from recent-task comparisons. Including it changes the multi-service count to 89/103 (86.4%); the 73-person recent subset stays unchanged.

“Îmi e mai dificil sa introduc datele în Excel, e că am multe itemuri dar după e ușor să-l consult”

R072 · survey answer J · reported check: 2–5 minutes, fairly easy

The consultation is easy; entering the underlying data is the burden. The questionnaire does not isolate that maintenance time.

“Doar excel. Am un template mai complex si imi ofera tot ce vreau.”

R019 · survey answer K

A personalised spreadsheet can already be the right solution. R100 also values the awareness gained by recording spending manually.

R084 reports checking a shared sheet in “2 seconds” once entries are kept consistent. R090 says cleaning imported internal transfers consumed almost as much time as manual entry. A fair comparison must include setup, collection, entry, corrections, reading and checking the answer, with one-time and recurring work reported separately.

Three behaviours, with different reasons to switch

Candidate segment

Tracking requires unwanted maintenance

Trigger: a periodic review, too much spent, or a question about subscriptions.

Workflow: check several apps, gather transactions and maintain a sheet or tracker. Personal categories and control are useful.

Observed consequence: R039 forgot entries; R053 found reconstruction too laborious and abandoned Excel; R072 struggles with entry.

Recruit only if this is still a recurring problem. R023, R031, R039, R053, R072 illustrate this behaviour; they are not a counted market segment.

Successful alternative

A personal system already works

Trigger: routine budget checks and deliberate financial control.

Workflow: an established sheet, template or app. Flexibility, shared access, history and discipline matter.

Observed result: R019 has everything needed; R084 checks a shared sheet quickly; R100 values manual attention.

Use a satisfied participant as a comparison case. Maintaining data is not automatically a problem to remove.

Lower initial priority

The bank app or a simple rule is enough

Trigger: salary arrival, curiosity or checking available money.

Workflow: consult a balance or chart, pay obligations and allocate the remainder.

Observed result: R015 finds the function easily; R043 values existing categories. Other respondents do not need a detailed consolidated report.

Do not interpret low tracking effort as poor money management or latent demand for SFA.

05

Online comments, classroom feedback & alternatives

Qualitative context

What the online comments add

They describe concrete questions: recurring spending excluding certain transfers, the full cost of a car, future instalments, and combining bank transactions with cash or shared spending.

They also challenge the opportunity. One commenter keeps Notes plus Excel; another prefers Money in Motion after comparing apps; another manages money through a salary-allocation rule.

The supplied file mixes platforms and includes uncertain attribution. Repeated posts by one author are not independent users. No comment is added to the survey denominator.

Team-reported discussion · 29 Oct

What the classroom changed

The professor asked us to investigate what users already do in banking apps, including Revolut budgeting. A classmate reported difficulty finding a custom-period filter.

Revolut documents custom periods. This observation can indicate a discovery or usability problem; it cannot establish that the feature is missing.

Raised hands were not counted against a recorded attendance base. Positive reactions are interest, not evidence of adoption or payment.

What SFA would have to do better

We prioritise a practical outcome: update and verify spending across payment accounts for a chosen period, without counting own-account transfers as consumption and with refunds handled as agreed with the user. A secondary question is the cost of a holiday or car across categories. Neither is a unique feature claim.

How discovery changes the product direction

Our next step is a narrower test, using the existing MVP.

The candidate segment is people who use at least two accounts for payments, already try to track spending, and can show a recent recurring problem with gathering, correcting or maintaining their data. Having two accounts alone is not enough. This is a segment to investigate, not a validated customer profile.

08

Are we ready for Customer Validation?

Not yet for a repeatable sales test. We are ready for focused problem interviews and an observed MVP comparison.

The questionnaire identifies plausible workflows and useful counterexamples. It does not establish that SFA solves the problem better, that people will keep using it, or that they will pay. We need to verify those three steps separately.

HypothesisStatusWhat the evidence permits
People in our sample often use several services and check spendingSupported in this sample88/102 use 2+ services; 73/88 report a recent attempt. Not a Romanian market estimate.
Some workflows involve unwanted entry or correctionSupported as an exploratory problem15/102 mention manual effort, omissions or consistency; concrete examples need follow-up to establish current severity.
All multi-bank users need a new dashboardWeakened51/73 recent multi-service checkers describe the task as easy; successful sheets and bank apps are clear alternatives.
Custom periods, aggregation or AI alone distinguish SFAWeakenedRelevant features already exist. The practical outcome and full effort must be compared.

Proposed next step · 2–15 November

A five-person test with explicit decision criteria

  1. Recruit four candidate users and one satisfied comparison participant. Candidates use 2+ payment accounts and can show a recent recurring difficulty. Include a happy Excel or bank-app user. Use supported bank formats and report that limitation.
  2. Start with their own method. Ask for actual spending over a chosen recent period and one relevant breakdown, such as food or subscriptions. Agree on accounts, cash, dates, currencies, refunds and excluded own-account transfers before comparing totals.
  3. Repeat with SFA. Include obtaining statements, import and corrections. Record setup separately from recurring entry, maintenance, lookup and verification. Use a fresh update in week two to reduce the learning advantage of doing SFA second.
  4. Check the result independently. Reconcile against source transactions and participant-confirmed treatment rules. Use a pilot tolerance of 1 RON after an agreed currency rule, with no material omitted or double-counted transaction. AI summaries are not the reference calculation.
  5. Observe return use over two weeks. Record self-initiated updates or new questions. Day 7/14 debriefs and prompted openings do not count as independent returns. Compare a relevant existing app if a documented feature could solve the same task.

Reason to continue

At least 3 of the 4 candidate users obtain correct results, save at least 30% of recurring work and 5 minutes over a measured week, and return independently for two further useful updates or questions.

An operational threshold for a small pilot, not a statistically validated market estimate. Report setup time even if these recurring-work thresholds are met.

Reason to change direction

If only 0–1 of the 4 candidates benefits and returns, or a brief explanation of an existing app removes the problem, reconsider the segment or primary problem. Two successful cases are inconclusive and call for a narrower follow-up.

Material errors block the value claim. Diagnose and repair correctness before interpreting retention or price signals.

Interest, use and payment are separate: record positive opinions without calling them adoption; record independent repeat use; then, after experienced value, make a concrete optional 20 RON/month continuation offer. Two actual payments among the four candidates would justify a small sales experiment, not establish product–market fit. A promise to pay is not a payment.

09

Evidence & research limitations

Evidence is linked to the claim it supports. The public bundle contains de-identified quantitative fields, calculation rules, theme IDs and selected anonymised excerpts. It does not contain the complete personal free-text export or unreviewed recordings.

  • Research appendix — source inventory, definitions, all closed-question distributions, cross-tabs, coding register, selected excerpts, context notes and official competitor sources.
  • Questionnaire wording — the 13 original questions and the observed structured answer options.
  • Quantitative response extract (JSON) — coded rows R001–R102, with R103 separately marked; no free-text financial descriptions or precise submission timestamps.
  • Reproduction script — recomputes the core counts and distributions from the included extract using Python’s standard library.

Evidence still to add: the original form URL, distribution links or screenshots, and completed interview records with consented evidence. The supplied materials do not contain verified recruitment reach or a channel attribution for every response. These gaps are disclosed rather than filled with estimated numbers.

This is exploratory research from a voluntary sample. Submission identities were not independently deduplicated. Self-reported times, coded themes, external comments and classroom reactions have different evidential weight. The current conclusion is a focused next experiment, not validated demand for a paid product.
