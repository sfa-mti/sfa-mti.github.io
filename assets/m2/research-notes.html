Sources, scope and reproducibility

The supplied source file is Cum îți urmărești banii și cheltuielile_ (Responses) (2).xlsx. The first 102 rows were compared with SFA_M2_Analiza_chestionarului.xlsx. The new export contains an additional response, R103. R001 corresponds to spreadsheet row 2, R102 to row 103 and R103 to row 104.

The 102 comparable rows have unchanged quantitative answers. Four NA/N/A/n/a values in free-text fields were previously represented as blanks. No unique identity check is possible from this anonymous export, so “respondents” below means distinct submissions within the analysis snapshot, not independently verified unique people.

For recent-task statistics, include only “once”, “2–3 times” and “4+ times”: 83/102. Of these, 73 also report 2+ banks/services. Retain 16 “never” and 3 “do not remember” responses for eligibility/account count and qualitative analysis, but not as verified recent-task episodes. The total question does not specify all accounts. “Several bank apps” does not prove complete consolidation.

Use the de-identified extract with reproduce.py to recalculate quantitative counts. Download both to the same folder and run python3 reproduce.py. Structured values are kept in Romanian; free-text financial descriptions and exact timestamps are omitted. Unstructured method responses are represented by a boolean flag. The script reports the number of “other” selections separately and does not infer standard choices from free text.

Manual theme coding is documented below with definitions and row IDs. Selected quotations provide examples, not the complete text used for every classification. Full re-evaluation of all themes requires the privately retained original export. The original form introduction and exact recruitment-post URLs are not part of the supplied source materials and have not been reconstructed.

Recruitment is voluntary and online. All 102 submissions confirm the form’s combined eligibility/participation question. The file does not support a detailed distribution by income, occupation or age, a reach denominator, or a response rate. The original distribution links/screenshots need to be added by the team.

A historical complaint is not necessarily a current problem. Coding is interpretive; uncertainty and counterexamples are retained.

Online comments — separate qualitative material

Source: the supplied Answers.txt. It contains Reddit, Facebook-style replies and passages with uncertain boundaries or authorship. No original public post URLs were supplied with this evidence bundle. The following are paraphrases of that material, not new survey rows.

  • One author describes wanting recurring spending excluding selected transfers or loans, the total annual cost of a car, and food split between home and restaurants. Two posts by that author count as one qualitative source.
  • One Excel user describes sessions of roughly two hours, but the time window over which they recur is not specified. Another exports and filters Revolut data before using a personal sheet. Neither establishes a measured weekly maintenance cost.
  • A commenter reports comparing Money in Motion and Welney for about a month and retaining MiM. This is an example of an alternative that works.
  • Another is satisfied with Notes plus Excel. Another uses a salary-allocation rule and delays discretionary purchases when needed. These are counterexamples to universal demand for an additional tracker.
  • App builders discuss cash, shared expenses, future obligations, checking instalments and reconciling with statements. These are useful problem descriptions, but app builders are not automatically representative paying customers.
  • A custom-artifact comment resembles R085. The identity is not confirmed, so it is not counted as an additional independent respondent. A long passage about projects/instalments is not attributed to the preceding named commenter because its authorship is unclear.

The comments suggest context-based totals, interoperability and forward planning as interview topics. They do not validate a paid product, and their count is not added to N=102.

Classroom feedback — team-reported context

Source: the team member’s written account dated 29 October 2026. The account says the professor encouraged further investigation and asked what users already use in banking apps, such as Revolut budgeting. It also reports a classmate having trouble finding a custom-period filter.

Revolut officially documents custom periods. We therefore distinguish a missing function from a function the person did not find, or a documented function that fails the exact task. The reported encouragement and raised hands do not establish demand. There is no counted audience base, recording or independently verified transcript in this bundle. Assistant interpretations embedded in the source discussion were not treated as additional observations.

Interview material has not been incorporated. The three Fiki records remain placeholders until actual findings and agreed evidence are added.

Official alternatives and price sources

Reviewed on 1 November 2026 in the preceding research analysis. Documentation establishes advertised capabilities; it does not establish real-account correctness or usability. Availability, bank eligibility and prices may change.

  1. Revolut: spending and income analytics

    Custom periods/categories are documented. Internal own-pocket/own-account exclusions do not prove every cross-bank transfer is reconciled.

  2. Revolut: manage linked accounts

    Linked-account analytics are documented; the exact combined result still needs a participant task.

  3. Revolut: linking eligibility

    Eligible account in the user’s name and a supported bank in the app. Do not assume every Romanian account is supported.

  4. Revolut: Romania plans

    Standard free; Plus 19.99 RON/month; Premium 39.99 RON/month. A paid plan is not required merely because SFA’s proposed price is around 20 RON.

  5. Money in Motion: product

    Describes bank import, reports, cash and family use. Marketing statements are not a test of every bank or account type.

  6. Money in Motion: tutorials

    Internal transfers, filters, cost centres and holiday tracking are documented.

  7. Money in Motion: Romanian App Store listing

    Free app with in-app purchases; open banking: 29.99 RON / 3 months and 59.99 RON / 6 months, about 10 RON/month equivalent paid as a package. Android price parity and trial duration were not established.

  8. Wallet: bank connections

    Bank synchronisation is a Premium feature; verify the actual bank/account.

  9. Wallet: labels

    Up to five labels per record and automated rules are documented.

  10. Wallet: filters

    Filters include account/category/label; saved-filter availability differs by platform.

  11. Wallet: excluded records and transfers

    Transfers are excluded from totals. Refund treatment as income can differ from a user’s desired net-spending definition; difference is not automatically an accounting error.

  12. Wallet: Romanian App Store listing

    Free plus Premium. The reviewed listing displayed 27.99, 79.99 and 149.99 RON products without unambiguous durations for each; no monthly conversion is asserted.

  13. Wallet: provider terms

    Current subscription prices are shown in the app. Manual imports and supported formats are described by the provider.

  14. Microsoft: free web apps versus Microsoft 365

    Excel for the web is a free alternative with a Microsoft account. Desktop editions or external automation can incur costs.

  15. Google Sheets: Romanian App Store listing

    Free listing. An existing personal sheet may already be automated and effective.

SFA’s approximately 20 RON/month proposal is untested. The supplied research does not establish acquisition cost, operating cost per customer or margin. A known feature list is not a competitive advantage: the next comparison must establish the same useful result with less full-cycle work.

Decision permitted by this evidence

Conduct a focused problem interview and MVP comparison with people who can demonstrate recurring friction in maintaining spending across payment accounts. Preserve successful spreadsheet and banking-app users as counterexamples. Do not claim broad Romanian demand, superior performance, retention or willingness to pay from the questionnaire.
